## Introduction
We ran this activity for our LABRATS summer program at Kerrisdale. In this activity, we taught students about acids and base, and students used radishes as a pH indicator.

The setup of this activity was average to high compared to other experiments. The cleanup was average compared to other experiments. We recommend using tablecloths to simplify cleanup.

## Materials

Here are all the materials we used:
- Radish juice
- Paper plates
- Cups
- Pipettes
- Various drinks: juices, alkaline water, vinegar, salt water, etc.

## Lesson

Topics in this lesson included learning about how acids and bases work (what they are, what are some examples and characteristics of each). Then, students learned about the pH scale and how pH indicators work.

## Activity 

At each table, we gave students cups containing the liquids, and also a cup of radish juice. Students were given paper plates, and using a pipette, they were to put a drop of the liquid on their plate and a drop of radish juice. Students recorded on a piece of paper the observed colour, as well as whether they predict it to be an acid or a base.

After, we switched liquids around and labelled it from A to D. Using the same method, students were asked to determine the acidity of these mystery liquids without knowing what they are. 

Note that it is important for the pipettes to not be contaminated for this experiment to work.
