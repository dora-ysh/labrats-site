## Introduction
This was the penultimate activity in our LABRATS summer program. In this activity, we taught students about how real-world water filtration works (where their water comes from, how it is processed in water treatment plants), and then we got students to make their own water filtration system.

The setup of this activity was average to high compared to other experiments. The cleanup was average compared to other experiments.

## Materials

Here are all the materials we used:
- Plastic bottles
- Coffee filters
- Cotton balls
- Activated carbon
- Rocks (2-5cm in diameter)
- Sand
- Gravel
- Scissors
- Elastics (to tie the coffee filters to the water bottle)
- Dirty water (mix water with dirt and instant coffee)

After doing the activity, we noticed that the rocks did very little to filter out anything, and is not an essential part of the activity.

## Lesson

Topics in this lesson included learning about the process of treating water, from rivers/lakes to homes. For example, we touched on sedimentation, filtration, as well as went over the materials used in filtration.

## Activity 

We gave students 2 attempts to make the best water filter. For the first attempt, we did not tell them what materials to put first and what to put last; we let students experiment with the materials.

Then, we had a class discussion, where we talked about what makes a good water filter (putting finer filtration materials, such as cotton and sand, at the bottom, and larger materials like rocks at the very top). Finally, students were given an opportunity to improve on their previous design.

We gave out a prize to the top 3 groups who had the best water filter.
